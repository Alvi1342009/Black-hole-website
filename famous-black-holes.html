🌠 Famous Black Holes

Across the universe, astronomers have discovered extraordinary black holes. Some helped scientists make history, while others are among the biggest or closest ever found. Let's meet these fascinating cosmic giants.

Meet the Giants

⬇

🌍 Meet the Universe's Most Famous Black Holes

Every black hole has a unique story. Some revealed new discoveries about our universe, while others challenged our understanding of physics. These famous black holes continue to inspire scientists and space enthusiasts around the world.

🌌 Sagittarius A*

Sagittarius A* is the supermassive black hole at the center of our Milky Way Galaxy. It has a mass of about four million Suns and is approximately 26,000 light-years away.

📸 Image Credit: Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration

📸 M87*

M87* became the first black hole ever photographed in 2019. It has a mass of around 6.5 billion Suns and lies about 55 million light-years from Earth.

📸 Image Credit: Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration

⭐ Cygnus X-1

Cygnus X-1 was one of the first strong black hole candidates ever discovered. It pulls material from a nearby massive blue star.

📸 Image Credit: NASA / ESA

🌠 TON 618

TON 618 is one of the largest known black holes in the universe. It has an estimated mass of about 66 billion Suns.

📸 Image Credit: ESO / NASA

🛰️ Gaia BH1

Gaia BH1 is the closest known stellar-mass black hole to Earth. It was discovered in 2022 and is about 1,560 light-years away.

📸 Image Credit: ESA / Gaia Collaboration

🌟 Did You Know?

📸 First Black Hole Photo

The first-ever image of a black hole (M87*) was released on 10 April 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration.

🌌 Our Galaxy Has One Too!

Our Milky Way Galaxy contains a supermassive black hole called Sagittarius A* at its center.

🌠 A Giant Among Giants

TON 618 is one of the most massive black holes ever discovered, weighing about 66 billion times the mass of the Sun.

🛰️ Closest Known Black Hole

Gaia BH1 is currently the closest known stellar-mass black hole to Earth.
