⚫ Types of Black Holes

Not all black holes are the same. Some are born from dying stars, while others contain billions of times the mass of our Sun. Let's explore the different kinds of black holes found across the universe.

Let's explore the four types

⬇

🌌 Not All Black Holes Are the Same

Black holes are classified according to their mass and the way they form. Scientists currently divide them into four main categories. Each type has its own fascinating story and plays an important role in understanding our universe.

⭐ Stellar-Mass Black Holes

These black holes form when massive stars run out of fuel and explode as supernovae. They usually have between 3 and 100 times the mass of our Sun.

They are the most common black holes discovered by astronomers.

📸 Image Credit: NASA Science

🌠 Intermediate-Mass Black Holes

Intermediate-mass black holes are larger than stellar black holes but much smaller than supermassive black holes. They are believed to have masses between 100 and 100,000 times the mass of the Sun.

Astronomers think these black holes may form when many stars or smaller black holes merge together inside dense star clusters. Although scientists have discovered several promising candidates, their existence is still being actively investigated.

📸 Image Credit: NASA Science

🧠 Did You Know?

Intermediate-mass black holes are often called the "missing link" because they help scientists understand how tiny stellar black holes might grow into enormous supermassive black holes.

🌌 Supermassive Black Holes

Supermassive black holes are the largest known black holes in the universe. They contain millions to billions of times the mass of our Sun.

Almost every large galaxy, including our Milky Way, has a supermassive black hole at its center. The black hole at the center of our galaxy is called Sagittarius A*.

Scientists are still studying how these enormous black holes formed and grew over billions of years.

📸 Image Credit: NASA Science

🌟 Primordial Black Holes

Primordial black holes are hypothetical black holes that may have formed just fractions of a second after the Big Bang. Unlike other black holes, they were not created from collapsing stars.

Scientists believe that tiny fluctuations in the extremely dense early universe might have produced these unusual black holes. If they exist, they could range from microscopic sizes to many times the mass of the Sun.

So far, no primordial black hole has been confirmed, but astronomers continue searching for evidence because they may help explain mysteries such as dark matter.

📊 Comparing the Four Types of Black Holes

Although all black holes trap light, they differ greatly in size, mass, formation, and where they are found.

🌟 Did You Know?

🌌 Every Galaxy Has One

Scientists believe that almost every large galaxy contains a supermassive black hole at its centre.

⚫ Tiny but Powerful

A stellar black hole can be only a few kilometres wide while containing several times the mass of our Sun.

🔭 The First Image

In 2019, the Event Horizon Telescope captured the first-ever image of a black hole: M87*.

❓ A Mystery Remains

Scientists are still searching for primordial black holes, which may have formed shortly after the Big Bang.
