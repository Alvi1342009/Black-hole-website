💡 Hawking Radiation

Can a black hole slowly disappear? In 1974, physicist Stephen Hawking proposed an astonishing idea: black holes are not completely black. They can slowly lose energy by emitting a tiny amount of radiation.

Let's discover Hawking's greatest idea

⬇

👨‍🔬 Meet Stephen Hawking

Stephen Hawking was one of the greatest theoretical physicists of the modern era. He studied black holes, gravity, and the origin of the universe.

In 1974, he combined quantum mechanics with Einstein's theory of gravity and proposed that black holes slowly emit radiation. This discovery became known as Hawking Radiation.

📸 Image Credit: wikipedia

⚛️ The Quantum World

To understand Hawking Radiation, we first need to visit the strange world of quantum physics. According to quantum mechanics, empty space is never truly empty. Tiny pairs of particles constantly appear and disappear for an incredibly short time. These are called virtual particles.

✨ Virtual Particles

Imagine empty space as a bubbling ocean of energy. Every moment, a particle and its opposite partner (called an antiparticle) suddenly appear together. Normally, they quickly meet again and destroy each other, disappearing without leaving a trace.

Near a black hole, however, something extraordinary can happen...

📸 Image Credit:wikipedia.

🌟 How Hawking Radiation Happens

1

Virtual Particles Appear

Two tiny particles suddenly appear near the event horizon.

2

Gravity Pulls One In

One particle falls into the black hole.

3

The Other Escapes

The second particle escapes into space.

4

Radiation Is Seen

To distant observers, it looks like the black hole emitted a particle.

5

The Black Hole Loses Energy

Because energy escaped, the black hole becomes slightly smaller.

⏳ Black Hole Evaporation

Every time Hawking Radiation escapes, the black hole loses a tiny amount of energy. According to Einstein's famous equation, E = mc², energy and mass are connected. As energy escapes, the black hole slowly loses mass. This process is called black hole evaporation.

🌌 A Very Slow Process

Large black holes evaporate incredibly slowly. A black hole with the mass of our Sun would take far longer than the current age of the universe to disappear completely.

As the black hole becomes smaller, it gets hotter and emits radiation faster. Eventually, in theory, it would completely evaporate.

🌟 Did You Know?

🕰️ Black Holes Live for an Unimaginable Time

A black hole with the mass of our Sun would take about 10⁶⁷ years to completely evaporate. That's far longer than the current age of the universe!

💡 Hawking Radiation Has Never Been Seen Directly

Scientists have not yet detected Hawking Radiation because it is incredibly weak for large black holes.

⚛️ A Revolutionary Discovery

Hawking Radiation was the first idea to combine quantum mechanics, gravity, and thermodynamics into one theory.

🌌 Tiny Black Holes Glow More

The smaller a black hole becomes, the hotter it gets and the faster it emits Hawking Radiation.
